I've spent three decades on calls and tickets — from frontline ISP support to
consulting engagements with nonprofits and enterprises. The technology changed
constantly. What worked to build trust with a frustrated customer didn't.

## People don't remember the fix. They remember how it felt.

A customer with no internet doesn't care about BGP routing or DNS propagation.
They care about whether you understand that their business stopped working the
moment their connection dropped. Lead with that understanding before you lead
with the diagnosis.

## The best troubleshooters ask, they don't assume

The fastest way to burn an hour on a ticket is to assume you know the cause
before you've actually listened to the full symptom description. I've solved
more problems by asking one more clarifying question than by jumping straight
to the usual suspects.

## Documentation is a gift to your future self

Every environment I've supported — small business, nonprofit, enterprise — got
easier to maintain the moment someone (usually me) wrote down what was actually
configured and why. It's unglamorous work. It's also the difference between a
30-minute fix and a 3-hour one, six months later.
