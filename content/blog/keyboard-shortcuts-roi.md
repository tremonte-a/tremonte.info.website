Every keystroke you save is small. Multiply it by the number of times you repeat
a task in a year and the math stops being trivial.

When I started outlining *Computer Aerobics*, I tracked how many times a typical
office worker reaches for a mouse to do something a shortcut could handle in
half the time — copy, paste, switch windows, save, undo. The number was higher
than I expected, and the annual time cost surprised even me.

## The compounding effect

A single shortcut might save you 1-2 seconds over reaching for a mouse. That
sounds negligible until you consider:

- The average knowledge worker performs these actions hundreds of times a day
- Context-switching back to a mouse breaks flow state, costing far more than the
  physical seconds
- Multiplied across a 5-day work week, 48 weeks a year, the savings land
  somewhere between 20-40 hours annually — a full work week, recovered

## Where I start with clients

When I train a small business team, I don't hand them a cheat sheet of 50
shortcuts. I pick the 8-10 that match their actual daily workflow and drill
those until they're automatic. That's the difference between a shortcut you
"know" and one you actually use.

This is the same philosophy behind *Computer Aerobics* — not an exhaustive
reference, but a trained habit-building system.
